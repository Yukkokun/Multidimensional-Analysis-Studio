The government announced a new package of measures on Tuesday, saying that the plan would help families cope with rising costs.
The prime minister told reporters that the proposals had been developed over several months and that they would take effect in April.
Opposition leaders criticised the announcement, arguing that the measures were too limited and that they had been rushed.
A spokesman for the treasury said the department would publish a full assessment later this week.
Analysts said the reaction in the markets had been muted, with investors waiting for further details.
The package includes changes to child benefits, a temporary reduction in fuel duty, and additional funding for local transport.
Officials confirmed that the scheme would be reviewed after twelve months.
Speaking outside parliament, the chancellor described the announcement as a turning point for the economy.
He said that ministers had listened to the concerns of ordinary families.
A report published yesterday suggested that the costs of the programme had been underestimated.
